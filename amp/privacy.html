Amp for Mac: Privacy Policy

Amp for Mac collects nothing. Nothing is recorded, and nothing leaves your Mac.

Last updated: November 9, 2026.

What the app does with sound

Amp for Mac listens to one audio input and plays to one output, in memory, sample by sample. It writes no audio to disk. The tuner and the Coach listen to that same input and keep nothing. macOS asks for the microphone because that is how macOS describes the input of an audio interface; the app cannot hear the interface without it.

The Ear, when set to the Mac's own sound, reads what the Mac is playing so it can find the key and the chords. macOS asks once for system audio recording. The Ear keeps the sound of the current listen in memory while you listen and discards it. What it saves is the result, as text: a title, the time, the key, the tempo, the chords and the sections. That list is stored on your Mac, in the app's own folder, and you can delete any entry in the app.

The camera

The Coach can use the camera to count the fingers on the neck of the guitar. This is off until you turn it on, and macOS asks first. The picture is looked at on your Mac and is never saved or sent anywhere.

Files

If you choose a cabinet impulse response file of your own, the app keeps a copy of that one file in its own folder so your choice survives a relaunch. Presets and settings are stored in the app's own preferences on your Mac.

What is collected, shared or sold

Nothing. The version of Amp for Mac sold on the Mac App Store makes no network connection of its own. There are no accounts, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking and no crash reporting by the app. Apple may share purchase and crash information with developers under its own privacy policy; that is Apple's service and not part of this app.

Children

The app collects no information from anyone, including children.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new date.
