Amp for Mac: Support

A guitar amp and a drum amp for the input of the audio interface you already own, with a tuner, a chord coach, and an ear for the key of a song.

Contact

Email keith.adler@icloud.com with questions, problems and requests. Say which Mac and which version of macOS you have, which audio interface, and what you did just before the problem. I read every message.

First run

Plug your audio interface in and open the app. It picks the interface for both input and output; change the menus at the top right if that is not your setup, and choose Guitar or Drums beside the power button. Press the power button. macOS asks once for the microphone, because that is how macOS describes an interface input. Allow it, then play.

Full help is in the app: choose Help, then Amp for Mac Help.

Common questions

I hear my guitar twice, or it sounds thin and hollow. Turn off direct monitoring on your interface. With it on you hear the dry guitar and the amp together.

I hear nothing. Check that the power button is lit, that the Input menu shows the interface and the right input (on a two-input interface the instrument jack is usually input 2), and that the meters in the grille move when you play. If macOS was not allowed the microphone, open System Settings, Privacy & Security, Microphone, and switch Amp for Mac on.

It squeals. A microphone near open speakers feeds back through any amplifier. Use headphones, or keep the speakers low and away from the microphone.

I hear clicks or crackles. Choose a safer setting in the Latency menu. The dropout count beside it should stay at zero.

There is a delay between the string and the sound. Choose a faster setting in the Latency menu, and use an audio interface instead of the Mac's own microphone and speakers, which are slower.

The tuner shows nothing. It reads while the amp is on, one string at a time.

The Coach names the wrong chord. Strum the whole chord clearly and let it ring. It hears strummed chords better than fast single notes.

Can I use it inside a recording program? No. It is a standalone amp, not a plugin. To record it in another app, send its output through a virtual audio device and choose that device as the microphone in the other app.

What you need

A Mac with Apple silicon running macOS 14 or later, and an audio interface with an instrument input, or any microphone.
